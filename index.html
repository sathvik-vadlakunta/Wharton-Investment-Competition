Overall Scoring Formula Step 4 — applied within each sector

Every stock is scored relative to its sector peers, not the full universe. A stock ranked in the 80th percentile for P/E means it is cheaper than 80% of stocks in the same sector.

Scorei = Σ ( wf × percentilef,i )
where Σ wf = 1 (weights sum to 100%)
percentilef,i — how to read it
0 = worst in its sector
50 = median
100 = best in its sector
Only stocks with a value for that metric contribute. If no data is entered for a factor, its weight is redistributed proportionally across factors that do have data.
Score color guide
65–100 — Strong buy candidate
40–64 — Neutral / research more
0–39 — Weak in its sector

Auto-Computed Metrics derived from 2-year daily price data in data/prices/TICKER.json

These are calculated in the browser the first time you rank — no manual input needed. Blue AUTO chips mean the factor is already live.

12-Month Momentum (used in Momentum factor)
momentum = Pt−22 / Pt−252 − 1
Pt−252 = closing price ~252 trading days ago (≈ 12 months)
Pt−22 = closing price ~22 trading days ago (≈ 1 month ago)
Skipping the last month avoids the short-term reversal effect documented in academic literature. Higher momentum = better rank.
Annualized Volatility (used in Low Risk factor — lower is better)
rt = ln( Pt / Pt−1 )

σ = √( Var(r) × 252 )
Daily log returns over the past 252 trading days are used to compute sample variance, then annualized by multiplying by 252 (trading days per year) before taking the square root. Lower volatility stocks rank higher in the Low Risk factor.
Maximum Drawdown 1Y (used in Low Risk factor — lower is better)
MaxDD = maxt∈[0,T] ( Peakt − Pt ) / Peakt
Peakt = running maximum of closing prices up to day t over the past year. Expressed as a positive decimal (e.g. 0.18 = 18% drawdown). Smaller drawdown = better rank.
1-Year Return (display only — not directly scored)
Return1Y = Plast / Pfirst in 1Y window − 1
Shown in the stock modal chart. Not fed into the score directly — momentum already captures price trend in a more academically robust way.

Manual Metrics by Factor enter via ✎ Data in the Screener — stored in browser localStorage

Source these from SEC EDGAR 10-K/10-Q filings, or services like Macrotrends, Wisesheets, or Yahoo Finance. Pull the most recent annual figure unless noted.

Quality factor
ROIC (%) — Return on Invested Capital = NOPAT / Invested Capital. Higher = better.
Gross Margin (%) — Gross Profit / Revenue. Higher = better pricing power.
Debt / EBITDA — Total Debt / EBITDA. Lower = better (inverted in ranking). Banks & insurers: substitute ROE.
Growth factor
3Y Revenue Growth (%) — CAGR of revenue over 3 years = (Revnow/Rev3Y ago)1/3 − 1. Higher = better.
EPS Growth (%) — YoY change in diluted EPS. Higher = better. Use TTM if annual data is stale.
Valuation factor
P/E Ratio — Price / Diluted EPS. Lower = better (inverted).
EV / EBITDA — Enterprise Value / EBITDA. Lower = better (inverted). More sector-neutral than P/E.
FCF Yield (%) — Free Cash Flow / Market Cap × 100. Higher = better (more cash per dollar invested).
Income factor
Dividend Yield (%) — Annual Dividend / Price × 100. Higher = better for income.
Payout Ratio (%) — Dividends / Net Income × 100. Lower = better (inverted) — lower means the dividend is more sustainable relative to earnings.

Percentile Ranking Procedure computed fresh each time you click "Rank"

percentilei = rank(vi) / (N − 1) × 100

rank(vi) = number of peers with a strictly worse value than vi
For metrics where lower is better (P/E, EV/EBITDA, Debt/EBITDA, Volatility, Max Drawdown, Payout Ratio), the rank is inverted: a stock with a lower value gets a higher percentile.

If fewer than 2 stocks in a sector have data for a metric, all are assigned 50 (median) for that sub-metric — not enough data to differentiate.

Factor-level score = simple average of available sub-metric percentiles. If all sub-metrics for a factor are null, the factor is skipped and its weight is redistributed proportionally.

Position Sizing — Inverse Volatility Weighting Step 7

wi = ( 1 / σi ) / Σj ( 1 / σj ) × stock sleeve %
σi = annualized volatility of stock i (auto-computed above)
Riskier stocks automatically receive smaller position sizes. The formula is used as a starting point — apply hard caps after:

Max single stock: 5–8% of total portfolio
Max single sector: 25–30% (including ETF look-through weights)
Number of stocks: 15–25 (enough to diversify, few enough to research)
Minimum position: ~1% (smaller adds tracking work without impact)
Stress test: estimate portfolio loss in a 20% market decline ≈ portfolio beta × 20%

Data Sources & Bias Warnings

Price data
Fetched via yfinance and stored in data/prices/. Auto-adjusted for splits and dividends. Snapshot date: when fetch_prices.py was last run — re-run to refresh before ranking.
Bias warnings
Survivorship bias: approved list only contains securities that exist today — past failures excluded.
Look-ahead bias: only use financials that were public on the trade date.
Overfitting: don't tune weights until historical results look perfect.
Fundamental data
Pull from SEC EDGAR (free) at sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar, or Macrotrends / Wisesheets for formatted tables. Save snapshots with the date pulled so scores are reproducible.
Rebalance schedule
Re-run rankings every two weeks (fits the competition window). Sell/trim triggers: thesis breaks, score drops below 40th percentile in sector, position drifts more than 25% above target weight, or client constraint changes.
